Methodology and sources

Where research comes from

Records were found by searching NASA’s Technical Reports Server (NTRS) and, where noted, journal papers with a DOI. Every item links to its original page.

How sources are checked

Each record is marked “Unverified candidate source”. Only abstract-level text and search excerpts were read. Detailed findings, page and figure references are not extracted yet. Open the original to verify.

Evidence labels

Experimental observation, quantitative measurement, model prediction, authors’ statement, and IGNIS interpretation are kept apart. Unknown values show “Not reported in retrieved source”.

Ranking

The Explore ranking is a weighted relevance score using placeholder estimates. It measures relevance, not scientific quality or safety.

AI assistant

It answers from the records in the library plus general science, labels what it infers, and should say when evidence is insufficient. It can still make mistakes.

Current limits

The library holds a small sample, not all of NTRS or the PSI database. Comparison Lab, cross-study insights and PDF extraction are not built yet.

Known gap: partial gravity

The PSI database has no partial-gravity combustion data yet. Moon and Mars gravity results are expected from LUCI and FM2. Until then, microgravity and normal-gravity results bound what to expect, and E-FIELD Flames mimics partial gravity with an electric field.

The science behind the flames

Flames without buoyancy

On Earth, hot gas rises and draws fresh air in, which makes a candle flame a flickering teardrop. In freefall nothing rises, so oxygen reaches the flame only by diffusion. The flame becomes a dim blue sphere.

Why oxygen is the key variable

Flammability depends strongly on oxygen concentration. Spacesuits hold 100% oxygen at 4.3 psi, against 14.7 psi at sea level on Earth. Moon habitats and rovers are expected to sit between, likely 30% oxygen or more at 10.2 psi or less. That is over 40% more oxygen than air, so materials burn more easily.

Airflow

Airflow also matters in microgravity. Ventilation can feed a flame or blow it out, so test conditions record flow speed alongside oxygen.

Three layers of fire safety

Prevention

Control ignition sources and choose materials using flammability limits. Studied in BASS, BASS-II and Saffire-I to -III.

Detection

Smoke behaves differently without gravity. SAME and SAME-R tested detection on the ISS.

Suppression

FLEX diluted the chamber atmosphere with carbon dioxide and helium, both of which suppress fire.

About and limits

How rankings work

Each investigation has four 0–5 scores. Your sliders weight them. In non-spacecraft habitats, high-oxygen and partial-gravity-relevant work gets a small bonus. The scores are my editorial estimates, not NASA ratings.

What this site cannot do

It is not connected to the live PSI database, so it holds no raw measurements. Fire safety for rockets and propellants is outside PSI’s scope. Do not use this site for real material or hardware decisions.

The assistant’s limits

An AI model writes the answers. It can be wrong or out of date. Check important claims against NASA’s PSI database and the published papers.

IGNIS AI is an independent prototype for the NASA Space Apps Challenge. It is not a NASA product, is not endorsed by NASA, and is not a certified fire-safety system.
